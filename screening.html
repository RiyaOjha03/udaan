How to interpret this screen

Uranium: The 30 µg/L value is the U.S. EPA maximum contaminant level for public drinking water. The percentile is an empirical comparison against the public dataset below; it is not a health-risk threshold. A low percentile does not establish safety, and a high one does not establish contamination from ISR.

The screen compares uranium (30 µg/L), arsenic (10 µg/L), nitrate as nitrogen (10 mg/L), fluoride (4 mg/L), selenium (50 µg/L) with federal MCLs. It also reports secondary/aesthetic exceedances for pH (6.5–8.5), total dissolved solids (500 mg/L), sulfate (250 mg/L), and chloride (250 mg/L); these secondary standards are generally non-enforceable federal guidelines. Local rules and site permits may be stricter.

ISR assessment needs baseline and downgradient samples, well construction and aquifer context, hydraulic gradients, repeated measurements, and site-specific permit criteria. This single-sample screen cannot identify source, trend, aquifer vulnerability, or future degradation.

Data source: USGS National Water Information System observations accessed through the EPA/USGS Water Quality Portal. The embedded model uses only reported uranium measurements from groundwater samples in µg/L. Records are observational and may include multiple dates and sampling programs.
